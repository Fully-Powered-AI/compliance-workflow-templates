## The outcome

The annual round of declarations stops living in an inbox and a memory. The company secretary sees every director and officer on one page: when each was asked, the date the form was due back, whether it has arrived and, if not, how many days it is past. A director who joined part-way through the year is checked for the declaration they were asked for on joining. Every gap in a returned form is listed, from an unanswered question to a missing signature, so it can be chased before the meeting rather than discovered in it.

Then the useful part. Every answer on every form is quoted word for word, and every organisation or person a director names is set beside the secretary's own list of current suppliers, customers and the next meeting's agenda items. Where a name matches, the pair is written as a question for the counsel or compliance lead the company names to decide, who settles it or takes it to the chair. The secretary walks into the meeting knowing which disclosed interests touch the business of the day.

It is a register of facts and questions, and it stays one. It gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines, and it sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. It never says whether an interest is a conflict, whether a director may vote, or whether the company complies with any law, rule or policy: the person who decides says that. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer. It names no law or rule from memory: if a form refers to one, the reference is quoted, marked to be verified by the lawyer, and used for nothing. It compares names only, so an interest with no match is not cleared and a match is not a finding. Reminders to directors who are late are copied from text the company approved and wait for the secretary's approval; nothing is sent, and no record is changed.

## Who starts the work, and when

The company secretary starts it when the annual requests go out, runs it as forms come back, and runs it again whenever a new director or officer joins and is asked to declare. It is worth one more run a week or so before each board meeting, with that meeting's agenda items on the matters list.

The person who decides on declared interests answers the questions: the company's counsel or compliance lead, named for each run, to whom the register is addressed. The chair hears from that person, never from the register directly. Where a form refers to a law, rule or case, a lawyer the company chooses verifies it, and where the person who decides is not a lawyer (a compliance lead may not be), questions of law still go to a lawyer, who decides and signs anything about what a law, rule or case means.

## What they supply

- **The settings:** the round, the date requests went out, today's date, the next meeting, the secretary and the person who decides.
- **The board and officer list,** as an export: each person asked to declare, with position, appointment date and any date they left.
- **The declarations log,** as an export: when each form was requested, the date it was due back, when it was received, its reference and how many reminders have gone.
- **The returned forms,** every page.
- **The blank declaration form,** written and approved by the company's counsel or compliance lead, so each answer can be checked against its questions.
- **The matters list:** current suppliers, customers and other counterparties with any other names they trade under, and the next meeting's agenda items with the parties each concerns.
- **The approved reminder text,** if reminders are wanted.

If an export's column headers are not the ones expected, the workflow template stops and asks which column is which, rather than guessing.

## What comes out

One register, headed as a draft for the person who decides:

- **The register:** each director and officer with their request, due-back date, receipt and status, and totals that add up.
- **Outstanding:** who is late and by how many days, who is not yet due, and who will not have declared before the next meeting.
- **For the secretary to check:** unanswered questions, missing signatures or attachments, people never asked, and records that do not agree.
- **The answers:** every answer on every form, quoted with its form and question, and the names in it.
- **Interests set beside matters:** agenda items first, then suppliers, customers and other matters, each with the answers whose names match it, shown as an exact match or as one name inside another. Names that match nothing are listed too.
- **Questions for the person who decides,** one per pair, each ending with the point that relevance is theirs to decide; any law or rule a form refers to, to be verified by the lawyer; and two lines confirming nothing has been left out.
- **Draft reminders,** held for the secretary's approval, and a check block saying what was read.

## What has to change for your company

Name the counsel or compliance lead who decides on declared interests, and the lawyer you turn to when a form cites a rule. Your counsel or compliance lead writes and approves the declaration form; the workflow template only checks each question has an answer. Decide what you call each round and from which date a new director is asked on joining instead. Build the matters list before each run, with the trading names and former names you know of: the template compares only the names it is given. Set the reminder text, who approves it and how many reminders go before the secretary takes it further, and map your exports' column headers once.

We sit with your company secretary and the person who decides, write down how the round really runs, and set it up in the AI product your company has approved: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Your company keeps control of how the work gets done.
