## The outcome

When counsel brings a new or changed rule to the table, the first question is always the same: what do our own records already say about each requirement, and where are the holes? This workflow template answers that question from the records, the same way every time, so counsel spends the meeting on judgment instead of on hunting through folders.

For every requirement counsel supplied, the worksheet shows the requirement word for word, then each sentence in your policies and procedures that holds the terms counsel chose, quoted with its document and section. Beside them sit the controls your own register already maps to the requirement, with their owners, and the evidence your log holds for those controls, each item marked inside or outside the evidence window. Every missing link becomes a numbered gap with a plain question for the compliance lead: no policy found, no procedure found, a document with no recorded approval or past its review date, no control mapped, a control with no owner, no evidence inside the window, evidence with no recorded location. Controls whose wording holds one of a requirement's match terms but that nobody has mapped are listed separately for the compliance lead to confirm.

This is preparation for a lawyer, and it stays preparation. It gives no legal advice and has not been reviewed by a lawyer. It interprets no law or regulation: it never says what a requirement means, whether it applies, whether it is in force, or whether a passage, control or piece of evidence satisfies it. It never says the company complies; a requirement with every link in place has a complete record, nothing more. It names no statute or rule from memory, counts no date, and writes no policy, procedure, notice or response, even for counsel to edit. It maps no control, closes no gap, and sends, files, signs, certifies and calendars nothing. Asked for a verdict, a deadline or what to do, it says only that the question is for counsel or the compliance lead, by name.

## Who starts the work, and when

The compliance analyst or the compliance lead runs it once counsel has a set of requirements in hand: a new or amended rule counsel has flagged, a regulator's request counsel has broken into requirements, or a periodic look at a requirement list counsel already keeps. The worksheet goes to a lawyer by name: in-house counsel or, where the company has no lawyer of its own, the outside counsel it instructs. It also goes to the compliance lead, who settles the record questions. A business never receives it as an answer.

It is not for finding requirements or working out what a law requires; counsel supplies the list. Nor is it for testing whether a control works or judging whether evidence is good enough.

## What they supply

- **Counsel's request:** the review's name, counsel by name, the compliance lead, who prepared the requirement list, the source and scope as counsel describes them, and any effective date counsel gives.
- **The run settings:** the evidence window and the review date.
- **The requirement list,** one row per requirement, with the columns Req ID, Requirement text, Source as supplied, Match terms, and optionally Status as supplied and Counsel's note. The match terms are the words counsel or the compliance lead chose for finding passages.
- **The document register,** with Doc ID, Title, Type, Version, Owner, Approved on and Next review, and **the texts** of the policies and procedures it lists.
- **The control register,** if you keep one, with Control ID, Control, Owner and Req IDs (the requirements your company has mapped each control to), and optionally Frequency and Doc IDs.
- **The evidence log,** if you keep one, with Evidence ID, Control ID, Description, Dated and Location.

If a column is named differently, the workflow template stops and asks which is which. It works only from what you supply, needs no connection to any system, and never asks for a password or a login code.

## What comes out

One draft, headed as a draft for counsel and the compliance lead, with a short note to the person who ran it on top:

- **At a glance:** each requirement on one line, showing whether a policy and a procedure were located, which controls are mapped, how much evidence is logged and in the window, and its gap numbers.
- **Requirement by requirement:** the requirement quoted, its source and status, the match terms, every located sentence with its document and section, the mapped controls and their evidence.
- **Gaps,** numbered, each with the facts and its fixed question for the compliance lead.
- **Questions for counsel:** for every requirement, whether it applies within counsel's scope and whether what was found is what it calls for; any requirement not marked final; and any rule a requirement mentions that is not on the list, marked to be verified by counsel.
- **Controls not mapped, and records not used,** for the compliance lead to confirm.
- **For counsel to confirm,** a checkbox list, and a **check** block of what was searched and what the worksheet does not say.

## What has to change for your company

Use your own registers' column names, and decide which document types count as policy and which as procedure. Agree with counsel who chooses the match terms for each requirement, and whether you keep them beside your requirement list for the next review. Set the evidence window, or one per kind of control if a single window does not suit weekly, quarterly and annual controls. Name who runs the worksheet, where the reviewed copy is kept, and who may see it. Confirm under your own policy that your policies, registers and counsel's requirement list may be used in the AI product you have approved.

We sit with the counsel and compliance people who do this comparison today, write down how they really do it, and set it up in the AI product your company has approved: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Your team keeps control of how the work gets done.
