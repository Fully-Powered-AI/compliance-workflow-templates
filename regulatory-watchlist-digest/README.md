## The outcome

Every period ends with the same page, built from your own watchlist. Each new item from your agreed sources sits under the topic it belongs to, with its title, its source, the date it was published, the stage the publisher gave it and its link. Its key sentences are quoted with their places, so counsel can see in seconds what it says without anyone's gloss on it. A rule, a comment date or an effective date mentioned in the text is quoted on its own line, never counted and never explained.

Nothing quietly drops out. A source that was not checked for the whole period is listed, with the topics it leaves uncovered. An item with no text is listed. An item from a watched source that matched none of your agreed keywords goes to counsel to place, and anything set aside (an item already reported last time, one outside the period, one from a source that is not on the watchlist) is listed with its reason. The count at the end proves every item supplied is accounted for.

This workflow template prepares legal work for a lawyer to review. It gives no legal advice and has not been reviewed by a lawyer. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines, and it sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. It never says what a law or rule means, whether an item applies to your company, or whether your company complies, and it never names a law or rule from memory. It opens no link and runs no search: it reads only the text you give it. Counsel reads, decides what applies and signs the digest as read; the compliance lead assigns the follow-up.

## Who starts the work, and when

A compliance analyst, a paralegal in the legal team or the compliance lead starts it when the scheduled watchlist review is due, weekly or monthly, once the period's items have been collected from the agreed sources. The digest is always addressed to a named lawyer: in-house counsel, or the outside counsel you name if your company has no lawyer of its own. Without one, it stops and asks.

It is not for deciding whether a new rule applies to you, working out what a change requires, or writing a response to a consultation. Those stay with counsel.

## What they supply

- **The settings:** the period, today's date, the counsel the digest is for, the compliance lead, and who approved the watchlist and when.
- **The watchlist,** an export counsel approved: each topic with its agreed keywords, its sources and its owner. The default column headers are `Watch ID`, `Topic`, `Keywords`, `Source IDs` and `Watch owner`.
- **The source register,** an export of the sources counsel approved, marked as the issuing body or a third party, with the dates each was checked this period: `Source ID`, `Source name`, `Publisher`, `Address`, `Covered from`, `Covered to`.
- **The items found,** an export of what was collected, with the text pasted in: `Item ID`, `Source ID`, `Published`, `Title`, `Stage as labelled`, `Link`, `Text`.
- **The last digest,** optionally, so items already reported are set aside.

If a column header does not match, the workflow stops and asks which column is which. It never guesses.

## What comes out

One draft digest for the named counsel, headed with what it does not do:

1. **At a glance:** sources expected and checked, coverage gaps, and how many items are new, unmatched and set aside.
2. **New items by watch item:** each item's facts, the keywords it matched and where, its sentences quoted, its dates and any rule it names on their own lines, and any text in it addressed to an assistant, noted and ignored.
3. **New items with no agreed keyword,** for counsel to place.
4. **Coverage gaps:** sources not checked, or checked for part of the period, and items with no text.
5. **Set aside,** each with its reason.
6. **A reading list** for counsel to mark read and decide what applies, and for the compliance lead to record who follows up and how each gap is handled.
7. **A check** that every item is accounted for and that no law or rule appears outside a quoted sentence.

## What has to change for your company

The watchlist is the heart of it, and counsel owns it: the topics, the keywords that tie an item to each, the sources for each, and which sources count as the issuing body and which as commentary. Good keywords mean fewer items for counsel to place by hand. Decide the schedule, who collects the items and how, and where the signed digest is kept. Agree how the compliance lead records follow-up, and how many sentences to quote from each item. If your exports use other column names, change them in the template and the instructions together.

We sit with your compliance lead and counsel, write down how your watchlist review really runs, and set it up in the AI product your company has approved: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, with web browsing and search switched off for this workflow. It needs no connection to any system.
